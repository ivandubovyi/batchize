The red flags a YC application gets rejected for

This is the full list of what Batchize looks for, written out. It is generated from the checker itself, so it is what actually runs rather than a description of it.

The 8 that do real damage

These are the findings that change how an application reads, rather than how it sounds.

Buzzwords

Adjectives are what people write when they have not yet decided what the thing is. A partner skimming for the product finds a category instead, and moves on.

Caught in your writing: revolutionar… revolutioniz… revolutionis… disrupt… synerg… seamless… frictionless… innovat… leverag… empower… cutting edge game-changing game changer next generation scalable solution

Mission statements where a product should be

The question asks what the company does. An answer about why it exists reads as an answer from someone who has not built the thing yet.

Caught in your writing: our mission our vision we are passionate we're passionate we believe in a world on a mission to our purpose we envision

Signups presented as usage

Signups are the easiest number to grow and the least predictive one. Active, paying and returning are different words for a reason, and a partner converts the first into the second immediately.

Caught in your writing: waitlist signed up registered created an account interested

Answers that should contain numbers and do not

Some questions get skimmed for figures before they are read. An adjective where a figure should be is the most visible gap in an application.

Caught in your writing: how far along are you how many users revenue how will you make money

Competitors not named

Having no competitors reads as not having looked. There is always a substitute, even if it is a spreadsheet and a phone call, and naming it is what shows you understand the market.

Caught in your writing: we have no competitors nobody else does this we are the only

No lived experience where the question asks why you

Why this idea is really asking why you rather than anyone else. A description of the market does not answer it, however accurate the market description is.

Caught in your writing: when I was at my last job we interviewed I spent we shipped

Numbers that contradict each other across answers

This is the one nobody catches by rereading their own answers one at a time, and it is the one a partner cross-referencing finds first.

Caught in your writing: 23 customers in one answer, 40 in another revenue where you said no users

Timelines that do not line up

A launch date that does not match how long you say you have been building reads as carelessness at best. Partners assume the less generous explanation.

Caught in your writing: launched last month, building for two years

The 8 worth tightening

None of these sink an application on their own. Several together are what makes one feel unconvincing without a partner being able to say why.

Hedged claims

Hedging in writing usually marks a disagreement between cofounders that was never settled. Partners find the seam in the interview, so it is better to settle it now.

Caught in your writing: hopefully we think we believe we hope we feel we imagine trying to we aim to we intend to we plan to eventually

Weasel numbers

If you know the number, use it. If you do not know it, that is the more important finding, and a vague quantifier is how it hides.

Caught in your writing: some people a lot of people many people most people several companies various numerous industry experts studies show research shows

Unsupported superlatives

A claim to be first or best invites a partner to spend thirty seconds disproving it, and they will. Ordinary counting is fine; primacy claims are not.

Caught in your writing: the best the only first ever the largest the leading world's best unrivaled unmatched

Filler

Every phrase that carries no information costs you a line of a partner's attention, and attention on an application is measured in seconds.

Caught in your writing: in order to at the end of the day it is important to note it's important to note needless to say as you know basically essentially

Timing that pins down nothing

Recently and soon sound like progress and commit to nothing. A date or a duration is the same number of words and it is checkable.

Caught in your writing: recently soon shortly lately in the near future a while ago some time ago at some point

Passive voice hiding who did the work

It was built tells a partner nothing about which of you can build. In an application about founders, that is the sentence you least want vague.

Caught in your writing: was built were shipped has been developed is being designed

Percentages with no base

Three hundred percent growth is a perfectly good answer if you say growth from what. Without the denominator it reads as hiding a small number, which is usually correct.

Caught in your writing: 300% growth doubled month over month up 40%

The same sentence in two answers

Pasting one answer into another is visible immediately and suggests you ran out of things to say, which is rarely true and always expensive.

Caught in your writing: identical sentences across questions

The one you cannot catch by rereading

Reading your own answers one at a time is precisely how contradictions survive. A user count in one answer that disagrees with another, revenue claimed where you said you have no users, a launch date that does not match how long you say you have been building: each answer is fine alone and the pair is not. Batchize reads them against each other, which is what a partner does.
