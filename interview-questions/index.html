YC interview questions, and what each one is testing

The interview is ten minutes and the partners already read your application. What they ask next is usually the thing your application invited. These are the 29 questions worth being able to answer without hesitating, grouped by what they are actually probing.

Practise these out loud, not in your head. The gap between knowing an answer and being able to say it in one breath is the entire difference the interview measures, and it is invisible when you rehearse silently.

Product

What does your company do?

What they are testing: Can you say it in one plain sentence without warming up? This is usually the first question; a rambling answer sets the tone for the whole interview.

Walk me through what happens after I sign up.

What they are testing: Do you know your own product cold? Concrete user-level detail proves you built and use it.

What have you learned from your users so far?

What they are testing: Are you actually talking to users, and did anything you learned change the product?

What's the hardest technical part of what you're building?

What they are testing: Whether the founders understand their own difficulty, and whether a founder can answer without hand-waving.

What will you build next, and why that?

What they are testing: Prioritization. The best answers tie the next thing to something users asked for or a metric you're trying to move.

Traction

How do you know people want this?

What they are testing: The single most common interview question. Lead with your strongest concrete evidence: usage, revenue, retention, or direct user behavior.

How many users do you have, and how do you define a user?

What they are testing: Precision. A small, precisely-defined number beats a big vague one; fuzziness here reads as inflation.

What's your growth rate week over week?

What they are testing: Do you measure it at all? Knowing the number instantly matters almost as much as the number.

What's your retention like?

What they are testing: Whether people come back. If you don't measure it yet, say so plainly and give your best proxy.

Why isn't it growing faster?

What they are testing: Honesty under pressure. A specific bottleneck you're attacking beats an excuse or deflection.

Market & Competition

Who are your competitors, and why will you win?

What they are testing: Never say there are none. Name them, then give the one thing you understand that they don't act on.

Why hasn't this been done before?

What they are testing: Why-now insight: what changed (technology, regulation, behavior) that makes this possible today?

How big can this get?

What they are testing: Napkin math from the bottom up: price times plausible customers, not an analyst's top-down TAM slide.

What do people use instead of your product today?

What they are testing: If you can't name the substitute (even a spreadsheet), partners doubt the need exists.

What's the biggest risk to this company?

What they are testing: Self-awareness. Naming a real risk and your plan beats claiming there isn't one.

Team

Why are you the right people to build this?

What they are testing: Founder-market fit in one breath: lived experience, unique access, or proof of relentless execution.

How did you meet, and how long have you worked together?

What they are testing: Cofounder breakup risk. Evidence of having built things together matters more than years of friendship.

Who does what on the team?

What they are testing: Clear ownership. Overlapping vague roles suggest neither of you ships.

Who writes the code?

What they are testing: Partners want founders building the product. Outsourced development is a serious flag.

Are you working on this full-time? If not, when?

What they are testing: Commitment. A concrete condition for going full-time is acceptable; an open-ended maybe is not.

Business Model

How do you make money?

What they are testing: One simple mechanism, stated instantly. Complexity here reads as not having thought it through.

What do you charge, and how did you pick that price?

What they are testing: Whether you've actually tried charging. A price tested on real customers beats a price from a spreadsheet.

What does it cost you to acquire a customer?

What they are testing: Do you know your funnel at all? If it's too early, say what you do know: where users come from.

Why will this be a big business and not a feature?

What they are testing: The wedge-to-platform story: what you start with and what it expands into.

Insight & Why

Why did you pick this idea?

What they are testing: The origin story. Lived experience and specific observed pain beat opportunity-spotting.

What do you understand about this space that others don't?

What they are testing: The counterintuitive insight test. The best answers sound slightly wrong until explained.

What surprised you most since starting?

What they are testing: Are you learning? A real surprise proves contact with reality; no surprises suggests no users.

If this fails, why will it have failed?

What they are testing: Clear-eyed thinking. Founders who can name the failure mode are usually the ones avoiding it.

Why now, and why you?

What they are testing: The compressed version of the whole interview. Practice a 20-second answer that hits both halves.
